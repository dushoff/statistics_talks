Confounders

	You can never remove all the bias from an observational study

		ANS There will always be some difference between your groups that's out of your control

	But you can use statistical techniques to take some of the differences into account

----------------------------------------------------------------------

Confounders again

	Confounders don't bias a properly randomized controlled study

	But we still often want to take them into account

		ANS Otherwise, they can increase variability

	Best practice is to plan in advance to control for confounders
	expected to be important

----------------------------------------------------------------------

Variability

	The best way to reduce variability is \ldots

		ANS Have a bigger sample size

	Unfortunately, controlling for confounders can also increase
	variability

		A type of overfitting

----------------------------------------------------------------------

Summary

BC

	Seek truth by asking good questions

	Reduce variability by

		increasing sample size

		careful planning (avoid overfitting)

	Reduce bias by

		control and randomization when possible

		accounting for key confounders otherwise

----------------------------------------------------------------------

Validity

	Test by simulated experiments

	Baseline: in the counter-factual case where nothing is going on, you
	want to find nothing clear (no statistical significance) $1-\alpha$
	of the time (typically 95%)

	Better: true effect should be inside the confidence interval 95% of
	the time

	Best: the P-value histogram for the true effect should be flat

----------------------------------------------------------------------

Power

	Also test by simulated experiments

	If your hypothesis is right, what proportion of the time will your
	experiment work?

		Show a clear result with the right sign

		Or, show a clear “important” result with the right sign

	The first requires hypothesizing an effect size (or range of them)

	The second requires setting a second effect size as well!
