Observation noise

	Classically called observation error

		People don't feel sick

		Diseases get mis-diagnosed

		Doctors don't report

		Samples are imperfect

	This is what we estimated in our Harare and likelihood labs

----------------------------------------------------------------------

Dynamical noise

	Classically called process error

	Even if we know all the parameters in a simple model, we expect variation

		Random changes in the environment (environmental stochasticity)

		Random interactions between individuals

	This is what we modeled in our stochastic labs

		But we haven't tried to fit it yet

----------------------------------------------------------------------

Fitting different sources of noise is hard

	We can write a likelihood in the presence of observation noise

		By assuming dynamics are deterministic

	Or in the presence of dynamical noise

		By assuming observations are perfect

	villages)

	What if we have a large number of cases, and poor reporting (HIV in Harare)?

		ANS Ignore dynamical noise?

		ANS Can't really ignore either kind

TSEC Likelihoods

	Assume that the difference between the estimate $\hat y_i$ and the
	data point $y_i$ is normally distributed.  What is the log
	likelihood?

	$$L = \prod_i{\frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\left(\frac{-(
	y_i-\hat y_i)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)}$$

	$$\ell = \sum_i{-\log(\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}) - \sum_i{\frac{(
	y_i-\hat y_i)^2}{2\sigma^2}}}$$

	\emph{We maximize the likelihood by minimizing the sum of squares}

		and then solving for $\sigma$

----------------------------------------------------------------------

Least squares $\to$ likelihood

	Attaching your least squares fit to a likelihood means:

		You can \emph{use it} for statistical inference (LRT)

		You can \emph{challenge} the assumptions

Latent variables

	We model \emph{observed} variables:

		e.g., reported cases, estimated prevalence

	Using \emph{unobserved} latent variables

		true cases, true prevalence, true number of susceptibles

Requires a framework that can address the fact that our latent variables have many possible values

	Bayesian prior (stan)

	Integration (usually approximate) (tmb)

Multi-parameter inference

	Modern methods are already hard, and when you consider various sources of uncertainty, you're really on the bleeding edge

	Many high-profile models for Ebola (WA 2014), failed to consider dynamical
	noise.

	The biggest paper talking about dynamical noise neglected uncertainty in
	generation intervals

	Once you do multi-parameter inference, you may find that confidence
	intervals are very large -- this may reflect the reality of knowledge, but
	may not make you look good

SEC Paradigms

----------------------------------------------------------------------

Likelihood

	A powerful tool for evaluating models

	Need to question your assumptions

		Your likelihood isn't perfect, are you relying on it too heavily?

	Frequentist approaches (including maximum likelihood) almost always depend
	on calculating (or approximating) likelihood

	Bayesian approaches including MCMC always depend on calculating (or
	approximating) likelihood

		Generally more powerful

		ANS Price is more assumptions

----------------------------------------------------------------------

OTHER Frequentist inference

	To do frequentist inference on these complicated likelihoods, we need to:

		estimate likelihoods

		find the maximum likelihood

		use the likelihood ratio test to find confidence intervals

	This is hard

----------------------------------------------------------------------

OTHER Bayesian inference

	To do Bayesian inference on these complicated likelihoods, we need to:

		construct prior distributions

		estimate likelihoods

		estimate the posterior

	Usually \emph{a little} less hard

		ANS The price is more assumptions

----------------------------------------------------------------------

Summary

	We need \textbf{dynamics} to understand links between processes and outcomes

		How do things work?

	We need \textbf{statistics} to understand uncertainty

		What can we learn from \emph{data}

	Combining these two is difficult, but progress is being made.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

Summary

	Making your fit into a likelihood clarifies assumptions and creates a
	foundation for statistical inference

	Accounting for both dynamical and observation noise is hard

		and not always necessary

	Stepping methods don't allow for observation noise

		latent variable methods can address this

	Shooting methods don't allow for dynamical noise

		filtering methods can address this
